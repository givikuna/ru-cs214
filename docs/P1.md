Needs to do the following:

- Coalescing free chunks
    - Consider a case where we repeatedly call malloc() and request 24-byte chunks until all memory is used up.
      We then free all of these chunks and want a 48-byte chunk. To fulfil this we need to coalesce two adjacent 24-byte chunks into a single chunk that'll have at least 48 bytes.
      Coalescing can be done by malloc(), when it is unable to find space, but it is usually less error-prone to have free() automatically coalesce free chunks.
- Alignment
    - Pointers to 2-byte data must be divisible by 2, pointers to 4-byte data must be divisible by 4, and so forth.
    - We will assume that the largest data our programs will use has 8-byte alignment.
    - To ensure that any object allocated by malloc() has 8-byte alignment, we will ensure that all offsets from the start of our heap are multiples of 8.
    - This means that allocations must also be multiples of 8: specifically, the smallest multiple of 8 great than or equal to the request amount.
    - Thus, a call to malloc() requesting 20 bytes must actually allocate 24 bytes.
    - Note that each chunk must have a length that is a multiple of 8, and its header and payload must also have lengths that are multiples of 8.
    - This means that the smallest possible chunk is 16 bytes.
